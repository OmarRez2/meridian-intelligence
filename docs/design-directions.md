# Design directions

Three genuinely different directions were explored. Compare them live at `#/directions`.

## A. Meridian (recommended, fully built)
- **Idea:** the supply chain as a planisphere, the rotating star chart navigators used.
- **Metaphor:** suppliers, the warehouse hub and customers are stars on one plate. The calendar rim is a 48-month time axis.
- **Type:** Cormorant Garamond (engraved display) and IBM Plex Sans/Condensed (data).
- **Colour:** cyanotype night, with brass for inbound, glacier for outbound, ember for risk only, and lavender for synthetic-data marking.
- **Hero:** title on the left; the tilted instrument on the right, engraving itself into view while the network unfolds out of the hub.
- **Motion:** one GPU particle system morphs through constellations → supply routes → refinement strata → radial month chart, under a damped scroll-driven camera. "Open the Observatory" dives into the hub, where the particles gather into a ring, and the workspace assembles outward.
- **Analytics:** a calm instrument panel. The sky dims behind it, with no 3D in charts.

## B. Manifest (study)
- **Idea:** the supply chain as its own paperwork: bills of lading, container stacks, customs stamps.
- **Type:** IBM Plex Sans Condensed at heavy weight, stencil-like.
- **Colour:** corrugated steel, safety yellow, container blue, customs red.
- **Motion:** stepped and mechanical: containers drop, stamps strike.
- **Analytics:** the most legible of the three; it reads like an operations document.

## C. Tidewater (study)
- **Idea:** the supply chain as a harbour: inventory is water level, demand is current, the reorder level is a mark on the wall.
- **Type:** IBM Plex Sans Light, very large.
- **Colour:** sea glass, deep harbour, teal, buoy coral (light theme).
- **Motion:** continuous, fluid contour fields; gauges settle with overshoot.
- **Analytics:** beautiful for inventory, but strained for sales and supplier comparisons.

## Why Meridian
It's the only direction where the spectacle is itself an analytical object. The dial the visitor turns is a real time axis, and the morph from noise to strata to a chart is the project's method made visible. It meets the cinematic brief without leaking effects into the dashboard.

Manifest scored almost as high and is the right fallback if the team prefers something quieter. Its stamped "pending verification" findings and document layout would suit Meridian's future Engineering and Insights pages.
